TAM, SAM, SOM — Product Manager Context

TAM — Total Addressable Market
The entire market opportunity if your product could reach every potential customer.

Example:
You build a SaaS product for restaurants to manage online orders.
If 1 million restaurants could potentially use the software, the total revenue/opportunity from all of them is TAM.

Product Manager question:
“How big could this business become?”


SAM — Serviceable Available Market
The part of TAM that your product can actually serve based on geography, target customer, product capabilities, pricing, etc.

Example:
Your SaaS targets restaurants in India.
If 200,000 Indian restaurants fit your target market, that is your SAM.

Product Manager question:
“Which part of the total market can we actually target?”


SOM — Serviceable Obtainable Market
The realistic portion of SAM that your company can capture, considering competition, resources, sales capacity, budget, brand, and execution.

Example:
Out of 200,000 target restaurants, you believe you can realistically acquire 2,000 customers.
Those 2,000 customers represent your SOM.

Product Manager question:
“How much can we realistically capture?”


Simple way to remember:

TAM → Everyone who could need it
↓
SAM → People we can actually serve
↓
SOM → People we can realistically win


Why Product Managers use TAM, SAM, SOM:

1. Validate whether a product opportunity is large enough.
2. Define the target market and ideal customers.
3. Decide where to launch first.
4. Prioritize product features and market segments.
5. Communicate market opportunity with founders, investors, and teams.
6. Estimate realistic business growth potential.


Quick example:

Product: Restaurant Management SaaS

TAM: All restaurants that could use restaurant software
SAM: Restaurants in the target geography/segment that the product can serve
SOM: Restaurants the company can realistically acquire


Key idea:
TAM = Maximum opportunity
SAM = Targetable opportunity
SOM = Realistic opportunity
