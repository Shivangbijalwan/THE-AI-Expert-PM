INTRODUCTION TO ADVANCED PROMPTING

Standard prompting often fails at complex logic. Advanced reasoning techniques make Large Language Models (LLMs) think like expert problem-solvers by breaking down tasks, exploring diverse paths, and aggregating results.

==================================================

CHAIN OF THOUGHT (CoT): BREAKING DOWN THE LOGIC
==================================================
Concept:
Instead of asking an AI for an immediate answer, Chain of Thought (CoT) prompts the model to write out its intermediate reasoning steps explicitly before giving the final answer.

Why it works:
LLMs predict the next token based on context. Seeing step-by-step math or logic in the prompt primes the model to generate subsequent logical steps accurately.

How to Do It:

Zero-Shot CoT: Add phrases like "Let's think step by step."

Few-Shot CoT: Provide 1 or 2 examples of a problem solved step-by-step.

Example Prompt:
"A store sells apples in bags of 5. John buys 4 bags of apples. He eats 3 apples and gives 6 apples to his friend. How many apples does John have left?
Let's think step by step:

Calculate the total number of apples John bought.

Subtract the apples he ate.

Subtract the apples he gave away.

State the final answer clearly."

Key Tips:

Magic Phrase: Adding "Let's think step by step" to logic or math prompts drastically improves accuracy.

Structure: Outline the explicit steps you want the model to take for complex problems.

==================================================
2. SELF-CONSISTENCY: EXPLORING MULTIPLE PATHS

Concept:
Even with CoT, an AI can sometimes make a mistake in a single reasoning chain. Self-Consistency addresses this by generating multiple different reasoning paths for the same prompt.

How to Do It:

Submit the exact same CoT prompt multiple times (e.g., 3 to 5 times) either manually in chat or via an API.

Adjust the model's temperature higher (e.g., between 0.5 and 0.8) so it explores different phrasings and paths rather than always picking the most predictable next word.

Example Prompt Execution:
Prompt: "A bat and a ball cost $1.10 in total. The bat costs $1.00 more than the ball. How much does the ball cost? Think step by step."
(Submit this same prompt 5 independent times.)

Key Tips:

Temperature Adjustment: Keep temperature around 0.5 to 0.7. If temperature is 0, the model will output the exact same answer every time, defeating the purpose.

Diversity: Observe how the model approaches the problem differently across the runs.

==================================================
3. RUN MULTIPLE TIMES & AGGREGATE (MAJORITY VOTING)

Concept:
Once you have your multiple outputs from Self-Consistency, you collect all the final answers and use majority voting to find the final consensus.

Example Process / Workflow:
Imagine running the bat-and-ball problem 5 times with these results:

Run 1: $0.05 (Step-by-step logic was correct)

Run 2: $0.10 (Model made a mental math error)

Run 3: $0.05 (Step-by-step logic was correct)

Run 4: $0.05 (Step-by-step logic was correct)

Run 5: $0.10 (Model made a mental math error)

The Winner:
$0.05 appears 3 times, while $0.10 appears 2 times. By majority vote, $0.05 is selected as the correct, robust final answer.

Key Tips:

Majority Voting: Choose the answer that appears most frequently among the runs.

Inspect the Logic: Check if the winning answers also share the correct reasoning path, not just the final number.
