JTBD — Jobs To Be Done
1. What is JTBD?

Jobs To Be Done means:

People “hire” a product or service to help them make progress toward a goal.

A customer does not simply buy a product. They buy it because they want to achieve something.

Example:
People don't buy a smartphone only because it is a smartphone. They buy it to:

Take photos
Watch videos
Communicate
Track health
Use apps
Work or study
2. What is a Job?

A Job is the task or progress a customer wants to accomplish.

Simple formula:

Customer → Need/Problem → Job → Desired Outcome

Example:

Customer: Student
Problem: Needs to attend online classes
Job: Attend classes easily from anywhere
Desired outcome: Learn without interruption

3. Functional Job vs Desired Outcome

Functional Job: What the customer wants to do.

Desired Outcome: What result the customer wants from doing it.

Example:

Job: Take a photo
Desired Outcome: Get a clear, high-quality photo quickly.

Another example:

Job: Track health
Desired Outcome: Know my daily health/activity level easily.

4. How to Define a Job?

Ask the customer:

1. What do you want to accomplish?
→ Understand the customer's goal.

2. Why do you want to do it?
→ Understand the motivation/reason.

3. What problem are you trying to solve?
→ Understand the customer's pain point.

4. What outcome do you expect?
→ Understand what success looks like.

Useful JTBD Question

“What are you trying to accomplish, and why is it important to you?”

5. Example — Smartphone Customer

Customer A

Job	What they want	Desired Outcome
Job 1: Take photos	Capture moments	Clear and beautiful photos
Job 2: Play videos	Watch entertainment	Smooth and enjoyable viewing
Job 3: Track health	Monitor activity	Understand health/activity easily
Job 4: Communicate	Call/message others	Stay connected
Job 5: Work/Study	Use productivity apps	Complete work efficiently
6. Why JTBD is Useful?

JTBD helps a company:

Discover valuable solutions → Build the right product → Communicate the solution → Communicate value to stakeholders

The key question is not:

“What product should we build?”

Instead ask:

“What job is the customer trying to get done?”

Then design the product around that job.
