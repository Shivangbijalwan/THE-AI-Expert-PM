PRICING STRATEGIES

1. COST-PLUS PRICING STRATEGY

Definition:
Cost-plus pricing is a simple method where a company calculates the total cost of making a product or providing a service and then adds a desired profit.

Formula:
Total Cost + Desired Profit = Selling Price

Example:
Production Cost = $500
Overhead Costs = $200
Total Cost = $700
Desired Profit = $200

Selling Price = $700 + $200 = $900

In simple words:
First calculate the cost, then add profit to decide the price.


2. VALUE-BASED PRICING STRATEGY

Definition:
Value-based pricing sets the price according to how much value or benefit customers believe they receive from a product or service, rather than only looking at its production cost.

Key Concepts:
- Customer Value: Price depends on the benefits customers receive.
- Perceived Value: Customers may pay more if they believe the product is highly useful.
- Differentiation: Unique features, better quality, convenience, or better results can justify a higher price.

Example:
A software product may cost $50 to develop for a customer, but it can help a business save $1,000 every month. The company may charge $200 because of the value it provides.

In simple words:
Price is based on the value the customer gets.


3. COMPETITIVE PRICING STRATEGY

Definition:
Competitive pricing is a strategy where a company sets its price by looking at what competitors charge for similar products or services.

Key Concepts:
- Market Matching: Set a price similar to competitors.
- Lower Price: Charge less to attract price-sensitive customers.
- Premium Price: Charge more when the product offers additional value or better features.
- Competitor Monitoring: Businesses regularly check competitors' prices and offers.

Example:
If competitors sell a product for around $100, a company might:
- Charge $95 to attract customers.
- Charge $100 to match the market.
- Charge $120 if it offers better quality or additional features.

In simple words:
Price is based on what competitors are charging.


QUICK DIFFERENCE

Cost-Plus Pricing    → Cost + Profit
Value-Based Pricing  → Customer's Perceived Value
Competitive Pricing  → Competitors' Prices


EASY WAY TO REMEMBER

Cost-Plus → "How much did it cost us?"

Value-Based → "How much is it worth to the customer?"

Competitive → "How much are competitors charging?"
